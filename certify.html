Constitutional certification for AI in clinical, diagnostic, and health operations roles.

Healthcare AI is classified as high risk under the EU AI Act. The Joint Commission is expanding AI scrutiny for accredited hospitals. The FDA has issued guidance on AI enabled medical devices. Health systems, digital health platforms, and clinical AI vendors are now operating in a regulatory environment where independent governance certification is rapidly becoming a procurement and accreditation requirement.

The Healthcare Constitutional AI track maps each of the seven pillars to healthcare specific compliance requirements. HIPAA alignment for Data Sovereignty. Clinical transparency standards for Transparency of Contact. Audit trail requirements for Audit Accessibility. Revocation protocols for AI systems operating in diagnostic or treatment advisory roles.

This track is designed for: hospital systems, digital health platforms, clinical AI vendors, health insurance AI operations, telemedicine platforms, and medical device manufacturers deploying AI components.

Track II · Financial Services AI Governance

Constitutional certification for AI in lending, payments, risk, and compliance roles.

Financial services AI operates under overlapping regulatory regimes. FINRA 2026 GenAI Governance demands robust AI oversight. The CFPB is scrutinizing algorithmic decisioning in lending. Federal banking regulators have issued guidance on model risk management for AI systems. The EU AI Act classifies credit scoring and insurance pricing AI as high risk.

The Financial Services Constitutional AI track maps each of the seven pillars to financial compliance requirements. Model risk management alignment for Declared Governance Framework. Fair lending transparency for Transparency of Contact. Data residency and cross border data flow compliance for Data Sovereignty. Regulator access protocols for Audit Accessibility.

This track is designed for: banks and credit unions, fintech platforms, insurance AI providers, payment processors, wealth management platforms, regtech companies, and lending marketplaces.

Track III · Government AI Governance

Constitutional certification for AI in public sector and institutional roles.

Government AI deployment is accelerating across federal, state, and international bodies. The Albanian AI minister Diella has surfaced the governance gap that no existing framework addresses. The Government Constitutional AI track maps each of the seven pillars to public sector governance requirements: NIST AI Risk Management Framework, CISA guidance, FedRAMP alignment, federal acquisition regulation, GAO audit authority, and civil rights protections including Title VI and Section 508.

The vendor transition governance checkpoint is Lunara's procurement innovation. When 68% of agencies plan to switch AI vendors, a constitutional governance framework ensures the standard persists across transitions. The implementation changes. The governance does not.

The Standard

Seven pillars of constitutional governance.

Every certification track is assessed against the same seven pillars of the Lunara Constitution. Each pillar has defined criteria, a verification mechanism, and a corresponding record in the public Shield registry. Track specific requirements map onto each pillar to provide industry relevant assessment.

I
Verified Identity

Legal entity identity is formally established and publicly accountable.

II
Declared Governance Framework

The operational rules governing the AI system are documented and accessible.

III
Transparency of Contact

Any interaction initiated by an AI system discloses its institutional origin.

IV
Data Sovereignty

Data handling policies are explicit, limited, and subject to accountability.

V
Audit Accessibility

Governance records are accessible to legitimate counterparties and regulators.

VI
Revocability

Certification and operating authority can be revoked for cause, publicly and verifiably.

VII
Proportionality

Governance requirements scale with the risk and reach of the AI system.
