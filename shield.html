An accountability record, not a trust badge

When an AI agent acting for your business does something it should not, two questions come first: who stands behind it, and did anyone outside it check? Your own logs answer what happened. They cannot answer those, because you wrote them. A Shield entry can: a named reviewer's dated decision about your business, kept by someone else, revocable, and readable by anyone.

Your domain

How verification works

Seven steps, and a name on the fourth.

A machine can see a DNS record. Only a person signs the decision, and the record says who.

  1. You apply

    Business name, website, domain, registration number and a contact email. Applying is free: no payment is needed to be reviewed.

  2. We check who you are

    Your legal registration is checked against official records before anything is marked verified.

  3. You prove the domain is yours

    One DNS TXT record at your domain. It is the only technical step, and review starts as soon as it is seen.

  4. A named person decides

    A reviewer checks the evidence and signs their name to the decision. Nothing is approved automatically, by an AI, or on payment.

  5. Approved, refused, or sent back

    Only an approved application gets a public Lunara ID and an entry. A refusal can be appealed with more evidence.

  6. Your entry goes public

    Anyone, a customer, a journalist or another AI system, can look your status up without an account, and it always shows the true current status.

  7. It stays accountable

    An entry lasts six months and can be revoked if standards are later broken. Revocations are published as openly as approvals.
