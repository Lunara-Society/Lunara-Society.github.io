What an entry contains

A lookup by domain or by ID returns the same record: the status, the business, its domain, when it was verified and until when, and the reviewer who signed the decision. Public IDs take the form SHIELD-YYYY-NNNN, and the lunara_id field carries the same value.

A certification can lapse or be revoked. A revoked entry stays on the record with the reason, published as openly as the certification was.

Questions

Before you rely on it

Why does almost every lookup say “not registered”?

Because the register is new and lists only what a named reviewer has verified. “Not registered” means nobody has checked the business through Lunara yet. It says nothing bad about the business, and it should never be read as a warning.

Can an AI system query it?

Yes, the same way a person does: a public endpoint with no key and no account, described in the API specification and available as a tool on the MCP server. The answer is the same whoever asks.

What is the founding cohort?

The first businesses to be verified, counted by the register itself; the figure above is read from it live. Being in the cohort records when a business joined. It does not outlast the certification: an entry can still lapse or be revoked. What founding participation includes.

What happens when a certification is withdrawn?

The entry stays, marked revoked, with the reason. Removing it would let a business that lost its certification look like one that never had it, and a register is only worth querying if its withdrawals are as public as its approvals.
