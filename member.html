Certification Methodology

The full seven pillar assessment methodology used by Lunara reviewers. This is the implementation layer behind the public certification overview. Do not distribute outside your organization.

Pillar I: Verified Identity

Verification steps:

  1. Confirm legal registration number against the registration country public registry
  2. Verify contact email domain matches the declared website domain
  3. Check business name consistency across registration, website, and public records
  4. Cross reference with fraud databases and sanctions lists
  5. Issue public ID (SHIELD format) only after identity confirmed

Pillar II: Declared Governance Framework

Verification steps:

  1. Require submission of AI governance policy document
  2. Assess policy against Lunara Constitution seven pillar framework
  3. Verify policy covers model selection, deployment, monitoring, and revocation
  4. Check for human oversight mechanisms and escalation paths
  5. Flag gaps and issue remediation recommendations

Pillar III: Transparency of Contact

Verification steps:

  1. DNS TXT record verification at _lunara-verify.domain
  2. Public contact page accessibility check
  3. Verify accountable individual or team is named and reachable
  4. Test response time on declared contact channels
  5. Confirm domain ownership persists through certificate lifecycle

Pillars IV through VII: Data, Audit, Revocation, Proportionality

Full methodology document available in the Audit Templates section. Each pillar includes:

  1. Documented verification criteria with pass and fail conditions
  2. Required evidence types and submission format
  3. Reviewer checklist with scoring rubric
  4. Remediation guidance for common failures
  5. Recertification triggers and timeline

Member Exclusive

Vendor Transition Governance Guide

The implementation guide for the vendor transition governance checkpoint. This is Lunara's key differentiator and the primary use case for enterprise certification.

The Problem

When an enterprise switches from one AI vendor to another, the governance framework built for the first vendor does not transfer. Security configurations, audit logs, compliance mappings, and oversight mechanisms all need to be rebuilt. This creates a governance gap during transition that can last weeks or months.

The Lunara Solution

The constitutional governance framework is vendor neutral. It operates at the institutional layer, not the implementation layer. When an enterprise switches vendors:

  1. The seven pillar assessment persists regardless of which AI system is deployed
  2. The governance declaration transfers because it is constitutional, not technical
  3. Audit continuity is maintained through the public registry record
  4. Revocation authority remains with the institution, not the vendor
  5. Recertification triggers a delta assessment, not a full rebuild

Implementation Checklist

  1. Complete seven pillar assessment before vendor selection
  2. Include Lunara certification requirement in vendor RFP
  3. Map vendor specific controls to constitutional pillars
  4. Establish audit continuity plan before transition begins
  5. Execute delta assessment during transition (target: 72 hour window)
  6. Update public registry with new vendor implementation details
  7. Verify revocation procedures transfer to new vendor relationship
  8. Schedule 90 day post transition review

Contract Language Template

The vendor shall maintain Lunara Constitutional AI Governance Certification throughout the term of this agreement. In the event of vendor transition, the certification shall persist through the institutional governance framework rather than the vendor specific implementation. The vendor shall support delta assessment within 72 hours of transition notification and maintain audit continuity through the public Lunara Registry.
