The first job: is it real?

Evidence first, then judgement, always said out loud

Every check runs in two steps. The first happens on your phone, for free, and reads what the file says about itself. The second, with credits, looks at the content itself and weighs everything together.

Photos and screenshots

Reads C2PA Content Credentials, the IPTC “trainedAlgorithmicMedia” label that Google, Meta, Adobe and OpenAI use, and the prompt blocks Stable Diffusion and ComfyUI leave behind. Shows an error-level map, then examines hands, text, light and texture.

Video

Scans the file for generator labels (Sora, Veo, Runway, Kling and others), samples six frames across the clip and compares them for faces, lips and backgrounds that change when they should not.

Voices

Record a call on speaker or open a voice note. Lens measures bandwidth, noise floor, pitch and pauses, reads any credentials in the file, and checks what was said for the patterns of voice-clone scams. A synthetic voice made with ElevenLabs is identified by its own signed credentials.

Text

Looks at rhythm, stock phrasing and structure, and says “can’t tell” for short passages. People are harmed when their own writing is called AI, so Lens needs several strong signals before it leans that way.

Scams

Paste a message or share a screenshot. Lens names the pressure tactics (urgency, secrecy, gift cards, a link that does not match the sender) and gives one safe next step.

Scan with the camera

Point it at a screen, a poster or a printed photo. Without file data only what is visible counts, and Lens says so.

No detector can prove that something was or was not made by AI, and any app that tells you otherwise is selling certainty it does not have. Lunara Lens tells you what the evidence shows, how strong each piece is, and what to do next. When the honest answer is “can’t tell”, that is the answer you hear. That is the same standard this institution holds itself to in everything it publishes.

Protect what you own

The Lunara Mark: invisible in the picture, public in the register

Mark a photo before you post it. Lens writes an eight-character mark into the image itself, spread invisibly across hundreds of blocks, and records who registered it, when, and the SHA-256 fingerprint of the original in the Lunara register. Anyone can check any image with Lens or on the verification page, without an account.

What it survives

Re-saving as JPEG down to low quality, and cropping. Tested on every build against photographs, graphics and text-heavy images.

What it does not survive

Resizing, rotation, heavy filters and screenshots at a different scale. We say so in the app, because a mark sold as indestructible would be exactly the kind of claim we exist to check.

Also in the metadata

The same mark is written into the file’s XMP data with a link to its record, so any software that reads metadata can find the owner too.

Questions

Asked before anyone buys

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes. Open the app in Safari and choose Share, then Add to Home Screen. It runs full screen with the camera and microphone. The Android download is the same app packaged for Android.

What leaves my phone?

On-phone checks never leave it. When you use Rosario’s AI, the image, frames or text are sent to Lunara’s server for that one answer and not kept; what is kept is which action you used and its credits. Your memory, lists, notes and files stay on your phone unless you copy files to your own Google Drive. The full privacy policy.

Can it tell me for certain that something is fake?

Only when the file says so itself: Content Credentials or a source label declaring AI generation are statements by the tool that made it. Everything else is evidence of varying strength, and Lens tells you which is which.

What languages does it speak?

English and Spanish, for the interface, the voice, the voice commands and translation. More languages are planned.

How do credits work?

Every AI action has a fixed price in credits, shown in the app before you use it; if an answer fails, the credits come back. Monthly plan credits are used first, then bought ones. Everything on the phone is free. In the app, Credits shows your balance in plain words, like “about 40 AI checks”.

I paid by PayPal. How do I get my credits?

Sign in to the app with Google, open Credits, choose what you bought and enter the Transaction ID from your PayPal receipt. If that step ever fails, email lunarasociety@gmail.com with the ID and we add it by hand.

How do I delete my account?

In the app: Account → Delete my account. Or follow the steps on the account deletion page.
