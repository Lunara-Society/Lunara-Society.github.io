Self-generated evidence is not independent verification

An AI agent's own logs, an identity platform's audit trail and a vendor's self-assessment all record what a system did. They are produced and held by the party being judged, so they cannot settle the question a regulator, a customer or a court eventually asks: who stands behind this system, and did anyone outside it check? That needs a record someone else keeps, can withdraw, and anyone can read.

This is not an argument against logs. Keep them; you will need them. It is an argument about what they can prove, written because the people who will ask for proof are now asking.

Accountability is being assigned before anyone has agreed what evidence discharges it. That gap is where the difference between recording and verifying starts to matter.

What first-party evidence is good for

A great deal. Agent identity platforms now register each agent with a named human owner and a risk tier, apply policy as agents reach for tools, ask a named person to approve risky actions, and keep auditable records of what was governed. RSA's Agent ID, announced on 29 September 2026, is one of several (Business Wire). That is real control, and an organisation running agents without something like it is running them blind.

What such a record answers is: what did the system do, and under which policy? It is the evidence of conduct. Nobody else can produce it for you.

What it cannot prove

Four limits of a record you keep yourself

  • Its author is the party being judged. The operator runs the system that writes the record, stores it and could change it.
  • It is not public. Nobody outside can read it without the operator's cooperation, at the operator's pace.
  • It does not say who stands behind the system. A complete log can belong to a company that is not who it says it is.
  • Nobody else can withdraw it. There is no outside party able to mark it no longer true.

What an independent record adds

  • Someone else keeps it. The party being described does not hold the pen.
  • A named person decided. Not a form that approved itself, not a payment that bought the outcome.
  • It can be revoked. A status that can change without the subject's permission is the only kind that carries information.
  • Anyone can query it, a customer, a journalist, a regulator or another AI system, for free and without an account.

Using both

The two answer different questions, and an accountable organisation needs both answers. Keep first-party records of conduct; they prove what happened. Have an outside record of identity; it proves who you are and that someone other than you checked. And know which obligations actually bind you and when, rather than which ones are being talked about.
